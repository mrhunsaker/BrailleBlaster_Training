# Unit 2: Advanced Lessons

Unit 2 is for learners who have mastered the basics and now meet the full variety of real source material. It covers the many file formats BrailleBlaster can open, the conversion engine behind them, structures that need special treatment, and the newest braille file format.

## Lessons in this unit

14. [Importing Source Formats I: Markdown and OpenDocument](../lessons/14-importing-markdown-and-opendocument.md)
15. [Importing Source Formats II: LaTeX, XML, NIMAS, and EPUB](../lessons/15-importing-latex-xml-nimas-epub.md)
16. [Choosing an Import Workflow](../lessons/16-choosing-an-import-workflow.md)
17. [What Pandoc Does for BrailleBlaster](../lessons/17-what-pandoc-does.md)
18. [Advanced Formatting: Boxes, Plays, Poems, and Deep Lists](../lessons/18-advanced-formatting.md)
19. [The eBraille Format](../lessons/19-ebraille.md)

Suggested total time: about 11 hours.

**Prerequisite:** Unit 1, or equivalent experience.

## The big idea of this unit

> **Importing is not transcribing.** A file that opens is only a starting point. Every format carries a different amount of real structure, and your job is to find out what survived and fix what did not.

Lessons 14 to 17 build that mindset across seven formats. Lesson 18 applies the same principle to structures that look different in print than they should in braille: you preserve the *function* of boxes, plays, poems, and deep lists, not their appearance. Lesson 19 looks ahead to where braille files are heading.

## Unit outcomes

When you finish this unit you can:

- Identify a source format and say what BrailleBlaster can and cannot preserve from it.
- Tell general XML from NIMAS, and treat EPUB as its own workflow.
- Explain what Pandoc contributes and use "convert to HTML first" as a deliberate fallback.
- Format boxes, plays, poems, and deep lists by function.
- Explain what eBraille is and why it supplements BRF.

## Unit 2 checkpoint

Complete this task before moving on. It should take 90 to 120 minutes.

**The task.** Collect or create four short source files: a Markdown file, an ODT or DOCX file, a LaTeX file with one equation, and (if you can obtain one) an EPUB. Add a short poem or a play excerpt to at least one of them.

1. For each file, write the first three things you will check after import (Lesson 16).
2. Open each in BrailleBlaster and record what survived and what did not.
3. For the LaTeX file, verify the equation in the Braille View (Lesson 15).
4. Format the poem or play excerpt by function (Lesson 18).
5. Write a short paragraph explaining to a colleague when "convert to HTML first" is the right fallback (Lesson 17).

**You are ready for Unit 3** when you can explain, for each format, what you verified and how you fixed what was wrong.
