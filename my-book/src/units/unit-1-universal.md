# Unit 1: Universal Lessons

Unit 1 covers the skills that **every** BrailleBlaster user needs, whether you transcribe one worksheet a month or a textbook a week. It moves from finding your way around the program, through formatting text and structure, into mathematics, and ends with the tools that get finished braille onto paper.

## How the unit is organized

- **Lessons 1 to 6: Getting started and formatting.** Interface, importing from Word, emphasis, and the three most common structures: styles, headings, and lists.
- **Lessons 7 to 10: Mathematics.** From the basic switch that marks math, to complex equations, spatial layouts, and number lines.
- **Lessons 11 to 13: Instruction and output.** Adjusting contractions for learners, working faster with Find and Replace, and producing physical braille.

You may reorder lessons within a group. Do not skip ahead across groups, because later lessons rely on the habits in earlier ones.

## Unit outcomes

When you finish this unit you can:

- Move confidently between the Print, Braille, and Style views.
- Choose emphasis, heading, and list treatments that follow *Braille Formats 2016*.
- Decide what is math, flag it, and build complex or spatial math correctly.
- Adjust contractions on purpose and document why.
- Make safe bulk edits with Find and Replace.
- Set up a page and an embosser and produce a test emboss.

## Unit 1 checkpoint

Complete this task before moving on. It should take 60 to 90 minutes.

**The task.** Your instructor, or you, supply a one-page worksheet in Word format containing a title, two headings, a short paragraph with one italicized book title and one bold key term, a nested two-level list, and two math items (one inline and one displayed fraction).

1. Open the file in BrailleBlaster and inspect the imported structure in the Style View (Lesson 2).
2. Correct the emphasis, keeping only what a reader needs (Lesson 3).
3. Set heading and list styles, and check them with Breadcrumbs (Lessons 4 to 6).
4. Flag the math and build the fraction with the ASCII Math Hub (Lessons 7 and 8).
5. Use Find and Replace to fix one repeated error safely (Lesson 12).
6. Set the page properties for 40 cells by 25 lines and preview the pages (Lesson 13).
7. Write three sentences: one decision you were unsure about, what you chose, and why.

**You are ready for Unit 2** when your Braille View matches your intentions for every element, and your written note shows that you can justify at least one non-obvious choice.
