# Lesson 48: Job Intake and the Agency Style Sheet

> **Lesson at a glance**
>
> - **Unit:** Unit 5: Handbook (Supplemental)
> - **Suggested time:** about 2 hours
> - **Prerequisites:** [Lesson 33](33-what-makes-a-good-source.md), [Lesson 43](43-braille-formats-crosswalk.md)
> - **You will need:** A computer, and the sample files from the [Sample Files](../samples/index.md) page where a lesson uses them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Take in a braille job with a complete intake ticket, so nothing important is learned too late.
- Write a request for a better source, and a realistic estimate of time and volumes.
- Build an agency style sheet that records the choices the standards leave open.
- Keep a log of exceptions, so that a decision made once is not made again from scratch.

> [!TIP]
> **Templates:** the blank **Job Intake Ticket** and **Agency Style Sheet** are in the [Job Aids](../reference/job-aids.md), as Aids 10 and 11.

## Why this matters

Most braille problems that reach a student began at the front door. The file was a PDF and nobody asked for the original. The math code was never confirmed. The deadline was before the term, but the volume count was never mentioned. A **job intake ticket** asks the right questions once, in writing, before the work starts.

The second tool is the **agency style sheet**. *Braille Formats 2016* leaves many choices open, such as how to word a note or whether to use an optional heading. If every transcriber chooses differently, a student's books change from volume to volume. The style sheet records the agency's choices in one place, so that the work is **consistent**, and so that a new transcriber can follow it on day one.

## The Concept

### The intake ticket

A ticket is a short form that travels with the job from request to delivery. A good one has these parts.

| Part | What it records |
| ---- | --------------- |
| **Who and when** | The requester, the contact, the date received, the date due |
| **The reader** | The student, by **initials or an ID, not a full name** ([Lesson 49](49-file-management-and-student-privacy.md)), and the grade level |
| **The source** | File names and formats, where they came from, and the quality on the ladder ([Lesson 33](33-what-makes-a-good-source.md)) |
| **Permission** | Whether the material may be transcribed, and any restrictions on copies |
| **The code** | Braille code, grade, and math code ([Lesson 29](29-ueb-technical-and-numeric-material.md)) |
| **The format** | Paper size, interpoint or single-sided, and the embosser ([Lessons 13](13-paper-size-and-embossers.md), [45](45-page-geometry-and-volume-sizing.md)) |
| **The content** | Subject, and the features: math, tables, images, code ([Lesson 47](47-subject-area-decision-guide.md)) |
| **Special requests** | Uncontracted passages, extra descriptions, tactile graphics |
| **The estimate** | Print pages, ratio, braille pages, sheets, volumes, and hours |
| **Status** | Who is working on it, who proofreads, who approves, and when it was delivered |

### A good estimate

An estimate has three numbers, and each comes from a measurement.

1. **Size.** Print pages, the ratio from a five-page sample, and the volumes ([Lesson 45](45-page-geometry-and-volume-sizing.md)).
2. **Time.** Make a sample too: time how long the same five pages took, including structure, math, and proofreading. Then scale up, and **add time for the source problems** you know about. A flat Word file or a scan takes longer than a NIMAS file.
3. **Risk.** List what could go wrong: a source that must be rebuilt, math in pictures, a graphic that needs a tactile reader.

Give the requester a **range** and say what drives it.

### The agency style sheet

A style sheet records **decisions**, not rules. It sits between the standards (which allow choices) and the transcriber (who must make one). A useful one covers these areas.

| Area | Decisions it records |
| ---- | -------------------- |
| **Codes** | UEB, Nemeth, and when each applies; contracted or not |
| **Page setup** | Paper, margins, cells and lines, interpoint |
| **Headings** | The map from print levels to braille headings ([Lesson 5](05-heading-levels.md)) |
| **Lists** | Conventions for outlines and lists |
| **Emphasis** | What to keep and what to ignore ([Lesson 3](03-typeface-emphasis.md)) |
| **Notes** | The standard wording of Transcriber's Notes ([Lesson 22](22-transcribers-notes.md)) |
| **Images** | When to describe, when to make a graphic, and the description style ([Lessons 27](27-images-and-tactile-graphics.md), [46](46-image-description-and-tactile-graphics-resources.md)) |
| **Tables** | Preferred formats and when to restructure ([Lesson 25](25-tables.md)) |
| **Math** | Conventions for borderline cases |
| **Page numbers** | Print and braille numbering, and running heads ([Lesson 21](21-running-heads-and-page-numbers.md)) |
| **Volumes** | Sheets per volume, and how to break ([Lesson 23](23-toc-and-volumes.md)) |
| **Files** | Naming, folders, and retention ([Lesson 49](49-file-management-and-student-privacy.md)) |
| **Proofreading** | Who reads, how many passes ([Lesson 32](32-proofreading-and-quality-assurance.md)) |

### A worked example

A fictional agency, **Lakeside Braille Services**, records these choices. They are made up for this lesson and are not recommendations.

| Area | Lakeside's decision |
| ---- | ------------------- |
| Codes | UEB for text. Nemeth within UEB for math, unless the student's team says otherwise. Contracted, unless a learner is being taught contractions ([Lesson 11](11-contraction-relaxer.md)) |
| Page setup | 11.5 by 11 inches, 40 cells by 25 lines, interpoint. **Make Default** is set only by the lead transcriber |
| Heading map | Print title: Centered. First subhead: Cell 5. Second and deeper: Cell 7, unless there are four or more levels, in which case ask the lead |
| Emphasis | Keep bold and italic on defined terms and titles. Ignore bold in headings and in glossary entry words ([Lesson 31](31-glossaries-and-indexes.md)) |
| Highlighting | Transcriber-defined typeform 1, listed on the Special Symbols page, and only when the teacher refers to it |
| Standard note | For a figure description: *Description of the figure.* Added by the Description style |
| Images | Describe every informative image. Tactile graphics for maps and geometry, by request |
| Tables | Simple when it fits the page. Otherwise Listed, with the standard note |
| Volumes | At most 40 sheets, broken at chapter ends |
| Files | `YYYY-MM-DD_StudentID_Title_vN.bbx`. No student names in file names |
| Proofreading | Two passes by different people for books. One pass plus a spell check for worksheets |

### The exceptions log

No style sheet covers everything. When you make a decision the sheet does not cover, **write it down** in an **exceptions log** with the date, the job, the question, the decision, and the reason. Review the log each term. Decisions that recur belong in the style sheet.

## Step by step

### Take in a job

1. **Open a ticket** the moment the request arrives.
2. **Fill in what you know.** Mark every blank you cannot fill.
3. **Triage the source** ([Lesson 33](33-what-makes-a-good-source.md)) and ask for a better one if you can.
4. **Ask the missing questions** in one message, not one at a time. The four from [Lesson 47](47-subject-area-decision-guide.md) are the start.
5. **Make a five-page sample** and estimate size and time.
6. **Send the estimate** and the open questions. Do not start the long work until the code and format are confirmed.
7. **Update the ticket** at each step, and close it on delivery.

### Start a style sheet

1. **Copy the template** from the Job Aids.
2. **Start with what you already do.** Record your current choices before you invent new ones.
3. **Mark the open questions** and decide them at the next staff meeting.
4. **Date it, version it, and name an owner.**
5. **Review it** each term against the exceptions log.

## Practice

### Exercise 1: Read a vague request

This email arrives:

> *Hi, can you braille Ch. 4 of the science book for my student by next week? It's about energy. Thanks.*

### Tasks

1. List at least eight things missing from the request.
2. Write the message you send back, in one message.
3. Which three facts most change the amount of work?
4. Fill in the **estimate** section of a ticket using this sample: five print pages produced 14 braille pages, in 2 hours 30 minutes, and the chapter is 36 print pages. It is a Word file that needs its headings rebuilt, and you expect two hours of repair. The agency uses interpoint and 40 sheets per volume.

### Exercise 2: Settle a style question

Two transcribers at the same agency handle a glossary differently: one keeps the bold on the entry words, and the other removes it. Write the **style sheet entry** and the **log entry** that would settle it.

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1.**

1. Missing: **which book and edition**; **the page numbers** of Chapter 4; **the source file** (and its format); **which student's code** (and the math code); **contracted or not**; **paper and interpoint**; **whether images need description or graphics**; **the exact due date**; **whether the chapter contains math, tables, or code**; **permission**; **who the contact is**.
2. A good message asks all of these at once, politely, and offers a default: *"Unless you tell me otherwise, I will use contracted UEB with Nemeth for math, on 11.5 by 11 paper, interpoint."* It also asks for the **Word file** or a NIMAS file.
3. **The source quality** (a clean file or a scan), **the amount of math, tables, and images**, and **the code** (a change of code changes everything).
4. **Size:** 36 × 2.8 = 100.8, so **101 braille pages**, which is **51 sheets** interpoint, and **2 volumes** at 40 sheets per volume. **Time:** 2 hours 30 minutes for five pages is 30 minutes a page, so 36 pages is **18 hours**, plus **2 hours** of repair, about **20 hours**. Give a range, such as 18 to 24 hours, because the sample may not be typical.

**Exercise 2.**

- **Style sheet entry:** *Glossary entry words: ignore bold and other print typeforms, unless needed for distinction (foreign words, titles). Use the Remove Emphasis tool after applying the Glossary style, then restore any exceptions.*
- **Log entry:** *Date. Job. Question: keep bold on glossary entry words? Decision: remove, per Braille Formats and the agency style. Reason: standard practice, and consistency across volumes. Added to the style sheet.*

## Key takeaways

- A **job intake ticket** asks the right questions once: who, the reader, the source, the code, the format, the content, and the estimate.
- Use **initials or an ID** for the student, never a full name.
- An estimate has **size, time, and risk**, each from a sample. Give a range.
- An **agency style sheet** records the choices the standards leave open, so work is consistent across transcribers and volumes.
- Log **exceptions**, and promote the recurring ones into the style sheet.
