# Lesson 47: Subject-Area Decision Guide

> **Lesson at a glance**
>
> - **Unit:** Unit 5: Handbook (Supplemental)
> - **Suggested time:** about 2 hours
> - **Prerequisites:** [Lesson 22](22-transcribers-notes.md), [Lesson 25](25-tables.md), [Lesson 27](27-images-and-tactile-graphics.md)
> - **You will need:** A computer, and the sample files from the [Sample Files](../samples/index.md) page where a lesson uses them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Ask the right questions of a teacher for mathematics, science, social studies, language arts, world languages, computer science, and music.
- Match a subject's typical content to the BrailleBlaster tools and course lessons that handle it.
- Recognize what BrailleBlaster does not do, and refer the job on.
- Handle early-literacy, test, and mixed-subject material with the right questions first.

## Why this matters

The program does not change from subject to subject, but the **content** does, and with it the decisions. A chemistry chapter is full of formulas and units. A history chapter is full of maps and timelines. A Spanish worksheet is in two languages. A computer science lesson is full of code. Each raises questions that no menu answers, and a transcriber who asks them early saves the teacher and the student a revision.

This lesson is a **decision guide**: for each subject, the questions to ask, the features to expect, and the lessons that apply. It does not teach the braille rules for any subject. Those belong to the code books and to *Braille Formats 2016*.

## How to use this lesson

Find the subject you are working on, read its **questions**, and ask them **before** you start. Keep the answers on the job ticket ([Lesson 48](48-job-intake-and-the-agency-style-sheet.md)). The practice gives you six jobs to size up.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Most of the decisions in this lesson belong to the **student's team**, not to the transcriber: which math code the student reads, whether text is contracted, how much description is wanted. Ask. Do not assume.

---

## The Concept

### Four questions for every subject

Ask these before the subject-specific ones.

1. **Which braille code and grade does the student read?** (UEB or another code. Contracted or not.)
2. **Which math code, if any?** (Nemeth within UEB, or UEB Technical Material. [Lesson 29](29-ueb-technical-and-numeric-material.md).)
3. **What does the teacher want the student to do with this material?** (Read it, answer questions in it, or study its figures.)
4. **Is there an answer key, a test, or a related worksheet** that must match this transcription?

## By subject

### Mathematics

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Which math code does the student use? | The whole transcription depends on it |
| Does the student need **spatial** layouts (long division, matrices, number lines) or linear ones? | [Lessons 9](09-spatial-math-editor.md) and [10](10-spatial-number-lines.md) |
| Are there **graphs and geometric figures** the student must work from? | Tactile graphics ([Lesson 27](27-images-and-tactile-graphics.md), [46](46-image-description-and-tactile-graphics-resources.md)) |
| Is the source LaTeX, Word equations, or pictures of equations? | [Lessons 35](35-word-structure-with-styles.md), [40](40-latex-for-transcribers.md) |

**Tools:** the math toggle, the ASCII Math Hub, the Spatial Math Editor, the Number Line Editor ([Lessons 7 to 10](07-math-translation-toggle.md)). **Watch for:** pictures of equations, numbers that are really labels, and a hyphen that is a minus sign.

### Science

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Are there **chemical formulas and equations**? | Subscripts and arrows are mathematics-style notation ([Lesson 8](08-ascii-math-hub.md)) |
| Which **units and symbols** appear, and does the student's code treat them as math? | Degree, percent, and unit abbreviations need a consistent rule |
| Are there **lab tables, data sets, and graphs**? | [Lesson 25](25-tables.md) and tactile graphics |
| Are there **diagrams** of apparatus, cells, or circuits? | Often a description plus a tactile graphic ([Lesson 46](46-image-description-and-tactile-graphics-resources.md)) |
| Is there a **glossary** of terms? | [Lesson 31](31-glossaries-and-indexes.md) |

**Watch for:** a unit run into a number (*5cm*), a subscript typed as a small character, and a table that is too wide.

### Social studies and history

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Are there **maps, timelines, and charts**? | Spatial relationships, so consider tactile graphics |
| Are there **primary-source quotations**? | Block quotations and attribution ([Lesson 4](04-layout-styles.md)) |
| Are there **tables of dates and statistics**? | [Lesson 25](25-tables.md) |
| Are there **sidebars and biographies**? | Boxes ([Lesson 18](18-advanced-formatting.md)) |
| Are there **many images with captions**? | [Lesson 27](27-images-and-tactile-graphics.md) |

**Watch for:** a map whose *key* is the real content, a timeline that is really a table, and captions that carry facts.

### English language arts

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Are there **poems and plays**? | The right styles, and line numbers ([Lessons 18](18-advanced-formatting.md), [30](30-line-numbers.md)) |
| Are there **numbered lines** the teacher will cite? | [Lesson 30](30-line-numbers.md) |
| Are there **citations, footnotes, and bibliographies**? | Notes and their placement ([Lesson 22](22-transcribers-notes.md)) |
| Are there **vocabulary lists and glossaries**? | [Lesson 31](31-glossaries-and-indexes.md) |
| Do **typeforms carry meaning** (titles in italics, emphasis, foreign words)? | [Lesson 3](03-typeface-emphasis.md) |

**Watch for:** a poem pasted as prose, a play whose speakers run into the dialogue, and emphasis the author used for fun and not for meaning.

### World languages

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Which **language** and which **braille code** for it? | BrailleBlaster's translation settings include **US Spanish** and other codes ([Lesson 29](29-ueb-technical-and-numeric-material.md)) |
| Is the page **mixed** (English instructions and foreign text)? | Each passage may need its own treatment |
| Are there **accented letters and special characters**? | They must survive in the source ([Lesson 41](41-text-encoding-and-braille-characters.md)) |
| Is the student **learning** the language in braille, or reading about it in English? | It decides how much of the foreign text is coded |
| Are there **non-Latin alphabets**? | These need their own code and may be outside BrailleBlaster |

**Watch for:** accents lost in a PDF, a name mistaken for a foreign word, and a contracted English rule applied to foreign text. Confirm the rule with the code book and the teacher.

### Computer science

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Is there **program code**? | It must be exact: use Uncontracted or Direct ([Lesson 28](28-editing-the-translation.md)), and often a note |
| Does **indentation** carry meaning? | Cell positions and line breaks ([Lesson 26](26-controlling-the-page.md)) |
| Are there **addresses, file names, and commands**? | These are strings that must not be altered |
| Does the student read **computer braille code**? | It is a separate code with its own rules |
| Is there **mathematics** (algorithm complexity)? | [Lesson 7](07-math-translation-toggle.md) |

**Watch for:** a contraction inside a variable name, a smart quote in code, and code that wraps across a braille line.

### Music

BrailleBlaster is **not a music braille tool**. Music braille is a separate code with its own software and training. If a job contains music notation, **refer it** to a music braille transcriber, and transcribe only the surrounding text. Say so in the job notes.

### Early literacy and primary materials

Early literacy textbooks have **their own formatting guidelines**: BANA publishes *Guidelines and Standards for Transcribing Early Literacy Textbooks*. They govern things such as how much text goes on a page, the use of large print, and alphabets and number practice. A transcriber working with a young reader should read them before starting, and expect to use more descriptions and more whitespace.

### Tests, worksheets, and exercises

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Is this a **high-stakes test**? | The testing agency often has its own braille rules and security. Follow them, not the general guidelines |
| Are there **multiple-choice items**? | The format has its own rules in Braille Formats |
| Will the student **write in** the braille, or answer elsewhere? | It affects spacing and answer lines |
| Are there **answer keys** that must match? | The transcription of the item and the key must agree |

> [!WARNING]
> **Secure test materials** carry confidentiality rules. Do not share, post, or keep a copy outside the agency's procedures. See [Lesson 49](49-file-management-and-student-privacy.md).

## Step by step

### Size up a new job

1. Name the **subject**, and read its questions above.
2. Ask the **four questions for every subject**.
3. List the **features** you expect: math, tables, images, code, poetry.
4. For each feature, find the **lesson** and the **resource**.
5. **Write the answers** on the job ticket, and ask for anything missing before you start.
6. If the job is **outside the tools** (music, a non-Latin alphabet), refer it on.

## Practice

### Tasks

For each job, list the first three questions you would ask, the features to expect, and the lessons that apply.

1. A 9th-grade chemistry chapter with equations, a table of element data, and a diagram of an atom.
2. A US history chapter with a map, a timeline, and three primary-source quotations.
3. A Spanish 1 worksheet with English instructions, Spanish sentences with accents, and a vocabulary list.
4. A Python programming handout with code samples inside paragraphs.
5. A high-stakes end-of-year test, in print.
6. A music theory worksheet with a few staves of notation.

### Answer Key

1. **Ask:** which math code? Are the units math or text? How much description does the diagram need? **Features:** chemical formulas, a data table, a diagram. **Lessons:** [7](07-math-translation-toggle.md), [8](08-ascii-math-hub.md), [25](25-tables.md), [27](27-images-and-tactile-graphics.md), [46](46-image-description-and-tactile-graphics-resources.md).
2. **Ask:** is a tactile map wanted? Are the quotations to be set off? Is the timeline a table? **Features:** a map, a timeline, block quotations. **Lessons:** [27](27-images-and-tactile-graphics.md), [46](46-image-description-and-tactile-graphics-resources.md), [25](25-tables.md), [4](04-layout-styles.md).
3. **Ask:** which code for Spanish? Is Spanish read as coded foreign text? Do accents survive in the source? **Features:** mixed languages, accented letters, a glossary. **Lessons:** [29](29-ueb-technical-and-numeric-material.md), [41](41-text-encoding-and-braille-characters.md), [31](31-glossaries-and-indexes.md).
4. **Ask:** is the code to be exact? Does indentation matter? Is there math? **Features:** code in prose. **Lessons:** [28](28-editing-the-translation.md), [26](26-controlling-the-page.md), [11](11-contraction-relaxer.md).
5. **Ask:** does the testing agency supply braille rules and a secure process? **Features:** depends on the test. **Lessons:** follow the agency's rules, and read [49](49-file-management-and-student-privacy.md) for security.
6. **Ask:** is there a music braille transcriber available? **Features:** music notation. **Action:** **refer** the notation on, and transcribe only the text, saying so in the job notes.

## Key takeaways

- Before any subject-specific question, ask the four: **code and grade, math code, what the student does with it, and what must match.**
- The decisions mostly belong to the **student's team**. Ask.
- Each subject has features that point to specific lessons: **math** to Lessons 7 to 10 and 29, **science** to 8, 25, and 46, **languages** to 29 and 41, **computer science** to 28 and 26, **language arts** to 18 and 30.
- BrailleBlaster is **not a music braille tool**. Refer music on.
- **High-stakes tests** have their own rules and security. Follow them.
