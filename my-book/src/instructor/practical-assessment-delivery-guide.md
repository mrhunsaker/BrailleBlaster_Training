# Practical Assessment Delivery Guide

> [!WARNING]
> Use the same candidate instructions, source files, time limits, permitted resources, and clarification policy for every candidate. Any approved accommodation should preserve the competency being assessed while removing an irrelevant access barrier.

## Purpose

This guide standardizes delivery of the five-station [Instructor-Led Practical Assessment](../supplemental-practice/student-assignments/instructor-led-practical-assessment.md). It aligns the practical with the course's four instructional units and gives evaluators a repeatable sequence for setup, observation, scoring, and feedback.

## Competency map

| Assessment station | Course coverage | Competencies to observe |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Intake and source assessment | Lessons 2, 14–17, 20; Unit 1 and Unit 2 checkpoints | Source inventory, provenance, structure inspection, risk identification, clarification and hold decisions |
| 2. Import and semantic repair | Lessons 4–6, 14–17, 20 | Appropriate import workflow, semantic hierarchy, list/table relationships, source fidelity, conversion-loss handling |
| 3. Specialized content | Lessons 7–10, 25, 27, 29 | Math-context judgment, mathematical accuracy, table strategy, figure description/tactile decision |
| 4. Production control and proofreading | Lessons 21–24, 26, 30–32 | Pagination and volume planning, running heads, notes, defect severity, source comparison, release gate |
| 5. Handoff and oral defense | Lessons 20–32; capstone | Reproducible documentation, QC evidence, limitations, escalation, reasoned explanation |

This map is an assessment alignment aid, not a claim that one practical task exhaustively tests every objective in a lesson. For a course-completion decision, retain the existing lesson activities, unit checkpoints, and capstone evidence as complementary measures.

## Before the candidate arrives

1. Confirm the BrailleBlaster version and workstation configuration match the course environment.
2. Prepare a clean candidate workspace and preserve pristine copies of every specimen.
3. Select the same specimen set for candidates taking the same assessment form. If a substitute is needed, use an equivalent task and document the version.
4. Test that source files open and that the candidate can save and reopen a project.
5. Print or provide the candidate packet, observation checklist, score sheet, and results record.
6. Keep answer keys, evaluator prompts, and expected findings out of the candidate packet.
7. Confirm the assessment duration, permitted references, assistive technology, and any approved accommodations before timing begins.

## Recommended delivery schedule

| Segment | Time | Evaluator action |
|---|---:|---|
| Orientation and rules | 10 min | Explain evidence requirements, workspace, permitted resources, and stop conditions |
| Station 1 | 20 min | Observe intake and source decisions |
| Station 2 | 35 min | Observe import, repair, and source comparison |
| Station 3 | 30 min | Probe math, table, and figure reasoning |
| Station 4 | 35 min | Observe planning, proofreading, and release decision |
| Station 5 | 20 min | Review handoff and conduct standardized oral prompts |
| Candidate review / closeout | 10 min | Confirm submitted artifacts and explain next steps |

Total scheduled time: 160 minutes. Allow additional time where accommodations or assistive-technology workflows require it; do not penalize approved access methods.

## Standard administration rules

- Give all candidates the same written task, time allowance, and access to permitted resources.
- Clarify ambiguous wording consistently; record the clarification. Do not suggest a solution.
- Do not score speed unless a timed performance criterion was announced in advance and is genuinely job-related.
- Record observed behavior and artifact evidence, not impressions such as “seems confident.”
- If a candidate identifies a source defect and appropriately escalates it rather than guessing, treat that as positive professional judgment.
- If a critical defect is observed, document the exact artifact, location, consequence, and candidate's release recommendation.
- Preserve candidate originals and working copies separately.

## Scoring and outcome

Use the [Evaluator Score Sheet](assessment-rubrics/evaluator-score-sheet.md) and [Observation Checklist](assessment-rubrics/observation-checklist.md). Apply the published passing threshold consistently. The numeric score does not override the critical-defect gate.

A conditional result requires a written [Remediation and Retest Record](assessment-rubrics/remediation-retest-form.md). Retest with a fresh or equivalent specimen and reassess the competency, rather than merely asking the candidate to correct the original work.

## Debrief protocol

1. State the outcome and the evidence supporting it.
2. Identify demonstrated strengths before describing gaps.
3. Describe each gap as an observable behavior or artifact defect.
4. Distinguish candidate-controlled errors from source ambiguities or environment failures.
5. Agree on the remediation evidence and retest conditions, if applicable.
6. Give the candidate a copy of the completed results record.

## Records and quality control

Retain the candidate packet version, source specimen version, submitted files, observation checklist, score sheet, oral prompt notes, and results record under the organization's training-record policy. Restrict access to instructor-only answer expectations. Review scoring consistency periodically by comparing evaluator notes against the rubric; revise the assessment version before using changed tasks or thresholds.
