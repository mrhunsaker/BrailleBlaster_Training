# Assessment and Grading

## Three layers of assessment

1. **Formative:** the Practice Document and Answer Key in each lesson.
2. **Unit checkpoints:** an applied task at the end of each [unit page](../units/unit-1-universal.md).
3. **Summative:** the [Capstone](../capstone/capstone.md), scored with its rubric.

## Using the answer keys

- Ask learners to work the practice **before** reading the key.
- Treat the key as one defensible answer, not the only one. Many items in this course say so directly. *Braille Formats 2016* describes itself as guidelines for structural decisions.
- Grade the **reasoning** as well as the result. A learner who cites the right section and applies a different but consistent mapping has shown the skill.
- Where a lesson says "verify in the Braille View," require evidence, such as a screenshot description or a pasted ASCII line.

## Checks for understanding

Each lesson in the teaching notes ends with three observable behaviors. Use them as a quick observation checklist during practice. A learner who shows all three has met the lesson's objectives.

## Unit checkpoints

Score each checkpoint as **Not yet**, **Meets**, or **Exceeds**:

- **Not yet:** one or more elements are wrong in the Braille View, or the learner cannot explain a decision.
- **Meets:** every element matches the learner's stated intent, and at least one non-obvious decision is justified.
- **Exceeds:** the learner anticipates edge cases, documents decisions in a Transcriber's Note, and corrects a classmate's work constructively.

## Capstone

Use the rubric on the capstone page. Suggested weights for a graded course:

| Component | Weight |
| --------- | ------ |
| Capstone rubric score | 50% |
| Unit checkpoints | 30% |
| Participation and practice completion | 20% |

Adjust to suit your program. For a certificate of completion, a capstone total of at least 28 out of 40, with no criterion below 2, is a reasonable threshold.

## Common error patterns to look for

- Typing spaces or blank lines to fake indentation or spacing.
- Treating every hyphen or the letter x as math, or flagging none of it.
- Keeping all print emphasis, or none.
- Running Replace All without testing.
- Skipping the test emboss.
- Trusting an import, a generator, or an automatic page number without proofreading.
- Confusing print page numbers with braille page numbers.
- Building the TOC before inserting End of Volume markers.

## Academic integrity

Because many answers are defensible, copying is easy to spot: look for identical rationales and identical mistakes. Ask learners to explain two decisions aloud as a short oral check if you suspect shared work.
