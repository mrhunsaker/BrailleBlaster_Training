# Instructor Portfolio Review Guide

> [!CAUTION]
> Do not accept screenshots or polished exports as sufficient evidence on their own. Verify that the learner can trace the work to its source, explain consequential decisions, identify limitations, and reproduce the skill on a new task.

## Purpose and boundaries

Use the learner portfolio to evaluate demonstrated competencies across the course. The portfolio supplements, but does not replace, direct observation, the applied import assessment, the instructor-led practical, or the capstone. A polished artifact without traceable source comparison and verification is insufficient evidence.

## Review sequence

1. Confirm the learner's portfolio is identifiable and contains no material that should not be shared.
2. Sample evidence across all four units rather than reviewing only the strongest artifact.
3. Check that source files, working copies, project versions, and exports are distinguishable.
4. Compare selected claims with the artifact and its verification record.
5. Review skills sign-offs against the observable criteria; ask the learner to demonstrate any skill for which evidence is weak.
6. Review major project records and confirm that critical findings were closed and verified against the correct version.
7. Complete the course-completion checklist and record any approved exceptions.

## Evidence acceptance criteria

Accept evidence when it is:

- **Authentic:** attributable to the learner and consistent with observed work.
- **Relevant:** directly tied to a course competency or production requirement.
- **Traceable:** identifies source, version, working artifact, and final output.
- **Sufficient:** includes the reasoning and verification needed to judge the competency.
- **Current:** reflects the learner's present ability, with reassessment after significant remediation.
- **Safe to retain:** excludes confidential or personally identifying production information unless approved secure systems and policies explicitly permit it.

A screenshot alone rarely establishes source fidelity, translation correctness, or complete proofreading. Request text evidence, a reproducible check, or a live demonstration as appropriate.

## Sampling and oral verification

For each unit, sample at least one artifact that includes a non-obvious decision. Ask the learner to explain the source authority, alternative considered, chosen treatment, verification method, and residual risk. For the capstone, sample at least one import or structural decision, one specialized-content decision, and one release-control decision.

If evidence appears inconsistent or copied, do not infer misconduct from similarity alone. Ask the learner to reproduce a relevant task and explain the decisions. Record objective observations and follow the program's academic-integrity process.

## Rating and remediation

Use the sign-off sheet's N/O, D, M, and E ratings. Sign off only at Meets or Exceeds. A developing skill requires a specific practice assignment, expected evidence, and reassessment date. Reassessment should use a fresh or equivalent task so the learner demonstrates transferable skill rather than memorizing a correction.

Critical defects override aggregate scores and portfolio completeness. Do not approve a final production disposition while a critical defect remains unresolved. Record the defect, affected artifact version, containment action, correction, and independent verification.

## Records and final decision

Keep only records required by the program. Store them in approved systems with role-appropriate access and retention controls. Do not copy confidential client or student materials into a public repository or an unapproved portfolio.

The final course-completion decision should be based on the checklist, completed skill sign-offs, assessment outcomes, closed remediation, and capstone disposition. Course completion is not equivalent to employer authorization or independent release authority.
