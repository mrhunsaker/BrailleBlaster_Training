# Practical Assessment: Standardized Evaluator Prompts

Use these prompts during Station 5 of the practical assessment. Ask the same core prompts of each candidate. Select follow-up probes only where needed to clarify evidence; do not coach or disclose expected answers during the assessment.

## Administration

- Ask the candidate to point to the artifact, source passage, or log entry that supports each answer.
- Record the prompt, a concise summary of the response, and the evidence cited.
- Do not award credit for terminology alone when the candidate cannot connect it to the work.
- Accept a defensible alternative when it preserves content, meets the task requirement, and is supported by evidence.

## Core prompts

### Prompt 1 — Source authority and provenance

**Ask:** “Which source did you treat as authoritative for the content you imported, and how did you establish that? What would you do if two supplied files disagreed?”

**Evidence to listen for:** source inventory, version/provenance notes, comparison of conflicting content, and escalation rather than silent selection.

### Prompt 2 — Import fidelity

**Ask:** “Show one place where the imported structure differs from the source. How did you decide whether to repair it, preserve it, or raise a question?”

**Evidence to listen for:** source-to-output comparison, semantic reasoning, documented correction, and recognition of uncertain content.

### Prompt 3 — Mathematics

**Ask:** “Explain the context decision for this mathematical passage. How did you verify that the result preserves the intended mathematics?”

**Evidence to listen for:** identification of math boundaries, context-sensitive treatment, comparison against source, and checking signs, grouping, order, and meaning.

### Prompt 4 — Table or figure

**Ask:** “What information must a reader recover from this table or figure? Why is your selected treatment appropriate?”

**Evidence to listen for:** relationships and learning purpose, header/data integrity, meaningful reading order, figure description or tactile rationale, and no unsupported invention.

### Prompt 5 — Proofreading and release

**Ask:** “Choose one defect you corrected. Show the source evidence, explain the correction, and show how you verified the surrounding context. What would make you stop release?”

**Evidence to listen for:** location and severity, minimal correction, source comparison after correction, regression check, and a clear hold/escalation threshold.

### Prompt 6 — Handoff

**Ask:** “If another transcriber had to continue this job tomorrow, what would they need to know first?”

**Evidence to listen for:** filenames and versions, project state, completed checks, assumptions, unresolved questions, and responsible next actions.

## Optional standardized probes

Use only when the candidate's initial response leaves an evidence gap:

- “Where is that decision recorded?”
- “Which source element supports that conclusion?”
- “What could a reader misunderstand if this were handled differently?”
- “What did you verify after making that change?”
- “Which part is certain, and which part requires approval?”
- “What would you do if the source owner could not be reached before the deadline?”

## Evaluation guidance

Credit reasoning that is accurate, relevant, and supported by the submitted work. Do not require a particular phrase or workflow where more than one valid approach exists. A candidate who identifies an unresolved source ambiguity and documents escalation demonstrates stronger production judgment than one who guesses and produces a superficially complete file.
