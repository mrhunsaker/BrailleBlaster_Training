# Backmatter

This section collects the operating guidance that applies across the BrailleBlaster training curriculum.

## Course completion

A learner is ready to complete the course when they can independently:

1. Select and open an appropriate source format without losing source content.
2. Apply semantic structure, styles, and braille formatting appropriate to the assignment.
3. Use translation controls deliberately and explain why a correction is required.
4. Produce and inspect a braille preview and the required export.
5. Compare the output with the source and project specifications.
6. Identify, document, correct, and verify defects.
7. Hand off the source, deliverables, and quality records so another qualified person can continue the work.

Use the [course completion checklist](../supplemental-practice/student-assignments/course-completion-checklist.md), [capstone assessment](../capstone/capstone.md), and instructor assessment materials to record evidence. Attendance or completion of exercises alone does not establish production competency.

## Production and accessibility principles

- Preserve the original source and work on a controlled copy.
- Retain semantic structure wherever the source and target formats support it.
- Follow the applicable braille code, transcription standard, contract, and project specification; this course does not supersede those authorities.
- Verify meaning, structure, pagination, translation, and usability in the actual deliverable.
- Do not treat a successful export as proof of correctness.
- Record assumptions, unresolved questions, corrections, and reviewer sign-off.
- Protect learner, client, and production data. Use synthetic or approved sample material in demonstrations.

## Version and standards control

BrailleBlaster menus, workflows, and features can change between releases. Confirm the installed version and consult its current user guide when a label or behavior differs from a lesson. Experimental features must be identified as experimental and independently checked before production use.

Code rules and technical requirements also change. Confirm the current authority and edition for the assignment, including applicable UEB guidance, Braille Formats requirements, and project-specific specifications. When sources conflict, stop and resolve the conflict with the responsible transcriber, educator, or project authority rather than guessing.
