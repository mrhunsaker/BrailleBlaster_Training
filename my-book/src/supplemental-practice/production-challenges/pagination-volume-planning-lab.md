# Pagination and Volume Planning Lab

## Purpose

Practice making and revising a defensible volume plan using supplied content estimates. This is a planning exercise: actual page and sheet counts must be confirmed in BrailleBlaster and against the job specification.

## Fictional job specification

A school requests a braille edition of a science unit. The job packet states:

- Keep chapters intact where practical; do not split a chapter merely to equalize volumes.
- Place the contents information where it is useful and compliant with the supplied template.
- Use consistent running-head conventions across volumes.
- Keep front matter and preliminary pages in the sequence specified by the template.
- Verify all final page references after pagination changes.
- Record any unavoidable chapter split and provide navigation cues.

No maximum volume extent is specified in this exercise. Students must state any assumptions rather than inventing a mandatory sheet limit.

| Chapter 5: Human impact | 39 |
| Glossary and index | 22 |

These figures are deliberately approximate. They do not include final pagination changes caused by formatting, tables, graphics, or production requirements.

## Tasks

1. Propose a volume plan that respects chapter boundaries where practical.
2. Explain where the contents pages belong and how each volume will identify its contents.
3. Identify which preliminary pages must be repeated, relocated, or handled according to the provided template.
4. State how running heads distinguish volumes and content.
5. Describe how you will recalculate contents references after pagination changes.
6. Identify the evidence needed before locking volume breaks.
7. Revise your plan after adding a hypothetical 12-page accessible table/diagram section to Chapter 3.

## Change-control exercise

A late correction adds 4 pages to Chapter 2 and removes 3 pages from Chapter 5. Record:

- Which volume boundaries may be affected?
- Which contents entries and page references require regeneration or rechecking?
- Which running heads, preliminary pages, or volume labels require review?
- Who must approve the revised plan?

## Evidence required for sign-off

- Actual page count from the working project.
- Verified chapter start/end locations.
- Confirmed volume breaks and contents placement.
- Cross-volume references checked against final pagination.
- Proofreading record showing that revised pages were rechecked.

## Reflection

A sound volume plan is not simply a division into equal page totals. Explain how content structure, navigation, production specifications, and later revisions influence the final decision.
