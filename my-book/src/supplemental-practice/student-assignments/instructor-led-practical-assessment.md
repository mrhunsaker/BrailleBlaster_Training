# Instructor-Led Practical Assessment: Candidate Packet

## Purpose and conditions

This assessment measures whether a candidate can make defensible BrailleBlaster production decisions, execute core workflows, and verify the resulting deliverable. It is a performance assessment, not a speed test.

**Suggested duration:** 2 hours 30 minutes, plus evaluator feedback.  
**Permitted resources:** course materials, application help, and the supplied practice files.  
**Required practice:** work only on copies; preserve source files unchanged.  
**Candidate evidence:** saved project(s), source-to-output comparisons, decision log, defect log, and a concise handoff note.

The evaluator may clarify task wording but must not coach the candidate through a production decision. Record any clarification supplied.

## Station 1 — Intake and source assessment (20 minutes)

You receive a Word document and a second source-format specimen from the Energy in Our Community practice pack.

1. Inventory the files and identify the authoritative source for each content element.
2. Inspect structure, encoding, headings, lists, tables, equations, and graphics.
3. Identify at least four risks that could affect accessible reading or production.
4. Record questions that require a content owner or supervisor decision.
5. Recommend proceed, proceed with documented exceptions, or hold.

**Evidence:** inventory, risk register, decision and clarification log.

## Station 2 — Import and semantic repair (35 minutes)

Import one supplied source using an appropriate workflow. Repair the supplied flawed HTML or equivalent flawed source specimen in a working copy.

1. Preserve wording, sequence, and meaningful distinctions.
2. Apply semantic heading, list, table, and emphasis structure.
3. Identify conversion losses rather than silently guessing at missing content.
4. Compare the imported result against the source.
5. Save the repaired source and resulting project.

**Evidence:** working source, project, change log, and comparison notes.

## Station 3 — Specialized content decisions (30 minutes)

Review one mathematical passage, one table, and one figure scenario.

1. Identify math context and select an appropriate treatment.
2. Choose a table strategy and verify header/data relationships.
3. Decide whether the figure needs a description, tactile graphic, combined treatment, or documented omission.
4. Explain each decision in relation to the reader's task and source evidence.

**Evidence:** decision matrix, math verification, table check, and figure brief or rationale.

## Station 4 — Production control and proofreading (35 minutes)

Using the supplied production scenario:

1. Draft a pagination and volume plan, including running heads and page-numbering considerations.
2. Review a seeded-error excerpt and log each defect with location, severity, evidence, correction, and verification.
3. Check the corrected passage against the source, including surrounding context.
4. Identify unresolved issues and determine whether the file is ready for handoff.

**Evidence:** pagination plan, completed defect log, QA checklist, and release recommendation.

## Station 5 — Handoff and oral defense (30 minutes)

Prepare a handoff note that identifies:

- deliverables and filenames;
- source files and versions used;
- completed checks and evidence;
- known limitations, assumptions, and unresolved questions;
- any follow-up action and responsible role.

The evaluator will ask the candidate to explain two decisions and one risk. The candidate should point to evidence rather than rely on memory.

## Candidate submission manifest

- [ ] Original source files retained unchanged
- [ ] Working copies clearly named
- [ ] Import workflow and decisions documented
- [ ] Corrected project saved and reopenable
- [ ] Math, table, and figure decisions supported
- [ ] Pagination/volume plan supplied
- [ ] Defect log and verification evidence supplied
- [ ] Handoff note identifies limitations and open issues

## Critical stop conditions

The evaluator must record a critical defect if the candidate knowingly releases or recommends release with unresolved content loss, materially incorrect mathematical content, missing essential table data, misleading figure treatment, an unverified source substitution, or a serious proofreading regression. A numerical score does not override a critical stop condition.

## Candidate reflection

After the assessment, record one workflow strength, one risk you would manage differently on a live job, and one skill requiring further practice.
