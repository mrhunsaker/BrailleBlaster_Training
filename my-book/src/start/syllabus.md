# Syllabus and Pacing

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a learner can:

1. Navigate BrailleBlaster and manage its views, previews, and menus.
2. Apply emphasis, headings, lists, and layout styles correctly and consistently.
3. Transcribe mathematics using the Math Translation Toggle, ASCII Math Hub, Spatial Math Editor, and Number Line Editor.
4. Import Word, OpenDocument, Markdown, LaTeX, XML, NIMAS, and EPUB sources, and verify the result.
5. Use Find and Replace, the Contraction Relaxer, and other productivity tools safely.
6. Set up pages and embossers and produce a BRF.
7. Produce a multi-volume transcription with running heads, page numbers, Transcriber's Notes, a table of contents, and T-pages.
8. Explain transcription decisions in writing.

## Assessment

- **Practice documents and answer keys** serve as formative checks in every lesson.
- **Unit checkpoints** at the end of each unit page ask you to apply several lessons together.
- The **Capstone** is the summative assessment. The [rubric](../capstone/capstone.md#scoring-rubric) is on the capstone page.

Instructors will find grading guidance in the [Instructor Guide](../instructor/assessment.md).

## Standards the course follows

- Braille Authority of North America, *Braille Formats: Principles of Print-to-Braille Transcription, 2016*, for layout and formatting.
- *The Rules of Unified English Braille* for literary braille.
- The Nemeth Code and BANA guidance for Nemeth within UEB contexts, for math.

Section numbers are cited throughout the lessons so you can check the source yourself.
