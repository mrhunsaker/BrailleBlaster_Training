# Labs

The lessons teach one tool at a time. The labs ask you to use several tools together on a realistic job, which is closer to what production work feels like. Each lab uses [sample files](../samples/index.md) and stays inside menu paths and behaviors the lessons already teach.

## How labs differ from lessons

- A lesson has an answer key. A lab has **expected observations** and **debrief questions**, because real files rarely behave in exactly one way.
- Labs ask you to **record what you see**. If BrailleBlaster does something different from the lab's expectation, that is useful information. Write it down, check the [Troubleshooting](../reference/troubleshooting.md) page, and tell your instructor.
- Labs use the printable [Job Aids](../reference/job-aids.md) for logs and plans.

## A suggested rhythm for a lab session

1. **Brief (5 min).** Read the scenario and the "Before you start" box.
2. **Predict (5 min).** Write down what you expect BrailleBlaster to do *before* you open the file.
3. **Work (most of the session).** Follow the steps and keep the log.
4. **Compare (10 min).** Compare what happened with your prediction.
5. **Debrief (10 min).** Work through the debrief questions with a partner or your instructor.

Prediction before action is the most valuable habit in the series. A transcriber who can predict an import's problems works much faster than one who only reacts to them.
